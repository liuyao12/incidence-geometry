02 / Fomin–Pylyavskyy

Incidence
on surfaces.

A local relation.
A global reason.

A quadrilateral can encode an incidence. Glue the quadrilaterals into a closed oriented surface, and one relation is forced by all the others.

01 / Read a face

Two points.
Two lines.
One condition.

Label a face cyclically A, ℓ, B, m. Its black vertices are points; its white vertices are lines. Assume no adjacent point lies on its line.

Coherent face

The join AB contains the intersection ℓ ∩ m.

In homogeneous coordinates this is equivalent to

r = ℓ(A)m(B) / [ℓ(B)m(A)] = 1.

All four pairings are nonzero. The ratio is unchanged when any point or line representative is rescaled. Notice the distinction: an edge means non-incidence; the face records the incidence.

02 / Close the surface

All but one
forces the last.

Surface incidence theorem

Label a closed oriented, bicolored quadrangulation by projective points and hyperplanes, with nonincident adjacent labels. If every face except one is coherent, the remaining face is coherent too.

In higher dimensions, coherence means that the line joining the two points meets the intersection of the two hyperplanes. The theorem is not confined to the projective plane, nor to spherical surfaces.

The proof: every edge cancels

Orient the faces consistently. Every point–hyperplane pairing occurs once in a numerator and once in a denominator. Therefore

∏faces f rf = 1.

If all other factors are 1, the remaining factor must be 1. The local geometric equivalence then supplies the missing incidence.

In the exact-weight view, double one edge. Two face ratios change reciprocally. Their product remains 1. This view isolates the algebra; arbitrary scalar values are not claimed to have a point–line realization.

03 / A cube gives Desargues

Perspective from a point.
Perspective from a line.

Two triangles whose corresponding vertices are joined through O have three collinear corresponding-side intersections. In the surface language, this familiar theorem can be organized on a cube.

The cube also has a seven-to-eight interpretation: the three initial coherent faces determine a missing line, and the remaining faces are coherent. This completion statement is distinct from the all-but-one-face implication for a fully labeled surface.

Different surfaces produce different incidence patterns. Fomin–Pylyavskyy also recover Pappus using a torus. The surface is a proof diagram, not a rigid tiling in the projective plane.

04 / Replace the labels

What happens when
the vertices carry conics?

The previous chapter replaced point pairs and line pairs by conics. We can make an analogous change here: put a conic at every vertex, require double contact along every edge, and ask for concurrence of the four contact chords on each face.

The correct scalar is no longer a point–hyperplane evaluation. Contact itself supplies a relative scale between the two quadratic equations.
